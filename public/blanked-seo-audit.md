# Blanked: SEO Audit + Keyword Strategy

**Site:** https://playblanked.com
**Prepared:** 25 May 2026
**Scope:** Visual memory game (iOS), independent, genuinely free core. Honest scientific posture.

**A note on numbers:** I do not have GSC, Ahrefs or SEMrush access for this engagement. Every search volume below is an estimate based on SERP composition, the number and type of competing pages, autocomplete and "People Also Ask" patterns, and forum/Reddit engagement as a proxy. I have tagged confidence (HIGH / MED / LOW) on each. Treat the bands as directional, not precise. Where I am unsure something will work, I say so.

---

## 1) Site crawl summary

Blanked is a one-developer iOS visual memory game with a content site that punches well above its weight. Topology is clean and deliberate: a conversion-focused homepage; three structural clusters (compare hub with 7 competitor pages; ~10 audience landing pages; a 3-term glossary); a ~30-post memory-science blog; an interactive `/memory-test`; plus author, about, press and legal. Internal linking is genuinely good, not decorative; the ADHD landing page alone links out to four blog posts, three sibling audience pages, the memory test and the compare hub, with anchor text that reads naturally.

Technical state is strong and mostly already handled: correct canonicals, `en_GB` locale, full OG/Twitter with image dimensions, MobileApplication/Organization/WebSite/Person JSON-LD, BreadcrumbList and FAQPage schema, IndexNow, WebP banners. First impression: the foundation is better than 95% of indie app sites, and the honesty angle (sourced studies, explicit "narrow gains" caveats, the Lumosity-FTC lesson baked in) is a real, defensible differentiator that most competitors structurally cannot copy.

Brutal-but-fair verdict: the site is strong on quality and weak on coverage surface area. The content that exists is excellent; there just is not enough of it yet, and a few high-intent query types (the "free alternative" intent, the test-tool head terms, several specific use-cases) are completely uncovered. Growth here is about adding well-chosen pages, not fixing what is broken.

## 2) Keyword opportunity analysis

The unifying insight from the SERP reconnaissance: Blanked's two structural advantages, **"genuinely free, no subscription"** and **"honest, sourced, narrow-claims"**, line up almost perfectly with query intents that the incumbents (subscription apps) and the content farms (thin clones, download aggregators) cannot serve well. The opportunity is concentrated in four seams:

1. **"Free / no-subscription alternative" intent.** Blanked has `/compare/X` pages (comparison intent) but no `/X-alternative` pages (replacement intent). These are different searches. The "free Lumosity alternative" SERP is currently directories and one aggressive PWA; nobody owns it with a credible, genuinely-free product.
2. **Test/tool head terms.** "Visual memory test online" and its variants are owned by Human Benchmark and a swarm of thin clones. Blanked already has a real, well-built test page that is underused against these terms.
3. **Question / symptom keywords.** "Why do I forget things", "how to remember where I put things", "how to remember names" type queries are served by news outlets and tracker-gadget blogs, not by anyone connecting the honest working-memory angle.
4. **Specific use-cases and uncovered audiences.** A handful of audience and situational long-tails the site has not built yet.

I have avoided head terms ("memory game", "brain training") except where there is an unusual angle, and skipped anything Blanked already has a page for.

## 5) Backlink + authority opportunities

### Outreach (realistic given the angle)

1. **Indie/solo-dev and "build in public" communities and press.** The "one developer in Gibraltar, anti-subscription-factory" story is genuinely press-worthy in the indie-app niche. Targets: Indie Hackers (a transparent build/revenue post), the r/iosgaming and r/iosapps communities (launch/AMA framed as honest, not spammy), and indie-dev newsletters. The hook is the business model and the honesty, not the game mechanics. Realistic because the story is true and differentiated.
2. **Memory-science and skepticism-adjacent writers.** The honest "narrow gains, no broad transfer, we deliberately avoid the Lumosity mistake" posture is catnip for science communicators who normally criticise brain-training apps. Pitch `/does-brain-training-work` (once built) to skeptic blogs and cognitive-science newsletter writers as "the brain-training app that agrees with you". This is the rare app they could cite approvingly.
3. **ADHD adult communities and creators.** The ADHD page is honest and well-judged (it explicitly says training is an adjunct, not a fix, and to see a clinician). ADHD-adult newsletters, YouTubers and subreddits respond well to non-over-promising tools. Realistic because the content already respects the community's intelligence.
4. **Senior-living and "keep your brain sharp" publications.** Outlets covering healthy ageing want free, honest, no-subscription tools to recommend. The seniors page plus the ACTIVE-trial-honest framing is recommendable. Realistic because "genuinely free" removes the usual conflict-of-interest objection.
5. **Teacher / study-skills communities.** The students page and the active-recall/spaced-repetition content suit study-skills educators and subreddits (r/GetStudying and similar). The honest "most study techniques do not work" blog post is sharable into these communities.

### Link-bait content ideas (things people cite)

1. **A free, public visual-memory benchmark with anonymised aggregate data.** Blanked already runs `/memory-test`. Publish a periodically-updated data study: "We ran N visual-memory tests; here is the distribution of scores by age band" (honest, anonymised, no overclaiming). Journalists and bloggers cite original datasets, and it directly supports ranking the test head terms. This is the single best link asset available and it uses data Blanked is already positioned to collect.
2. **"Does brain training work? Every major study, honestly summarised."** A maintained, sourced reference page (Simons 2016, Klingberg, Engle & Kane, ACTIVE, Cortese, Sonuga-Barke). Position it as the citable summary. People link to the thing that did the reading for them, and Blanked's posture makes it credible rather than self-serving.
3. **The photographic-memory debunk (`/glossary/photographic-memory`).** A clean, sourced "true photographic memory basically does not exist" page is the kind of myth-correction that gets cited in forum threads and articles for years. High evergreen link potential, low build cost.

## 6) Quick technical SEO wins (excluding the already-implemented list)

In rough priority order. Some are confirm-then-fix because they cannot be fully verified from outside.

1. **`/memory-test` crawlable-text check (HIGH).** The interactive test will not be readable by Google. Confirm there is sufficient indexable supporting copy (intro, instructions, "what your score means", FAQ) carrying the target keywords; if the page is mostly a JS canvas with little text, it will not rank for "visual memory test" no matter how good the tool is.
2. **Glossary hub is thin (MED).** Three terms under-serves a glossary and limits internal-link anchor opportunities. Expanding to 6 (with the proposed entries) strengthens sitewide topical authority and gives the auto-linker more targets.
3. **Confirm FAQPage schema is not over-applied (MED).** FAQPage rich results have been heavily de-emphasised by Google for non-gov/health sites. It is fine to keep, but do not rely on it for SERP real estate, and make sure the marked-up Q&As exactly match visible on-page text to avoid a structured-data mismatch flag.
4. **Internal link depth to deep blog posts (MED).** With ~30 posts, the oldest/deepest are probably 3+ clicks from the homepage. Add cluster grouping on `/blog` and "related posts" cross-links (the audience pages already do this well; extend it post-to-post) so equity reaches the long tail.
5. **Image alt text on blog banners (MED, confirm).** WebP conversion is handled, but confirm every banner and in-body image has descriptive alt text (not just the filename). The Supabase-hosted banners are the ones to check.
6. **Open Graph image is a single dynamic default sitewide (LOW-MED).** `opengraph-image` appears identical across pages. Per-template OG images (one for compare, one for glossary, one for audience pages) would lift social CTR; not urgent.
7. **Supabase-hosted blog images on a different origin (LOW, confirm).** Banners load from `xbhewtoryhtguvlmodfi.supabase.co`. Confirm this is not adding meaningful LCP latency on blog posts; if banners are the LCP element, consider serving them through the Vercel/Next image pipeline on the primary domain or a CDN edge. Measure before acting.
8. **Confirm `hreflang` scope (LOW).** Spanish exists only for legal pages. That is fine, but make sure there are no stray `hreflang` references pointing at non-existent Spanish versions of English pages, which would throw GSC errors.
9. **Trailing-slash / canonical consistency (LOW, confirm).** Homepage fetched resolved to `https://playblanked.com/` while canonical is `https://playblanked.com`. Almost certainly handled by Next, but confirm one canonical form is enforced site-wide to avoid duplicate signals.

Note: I could not fetch `/sitemap.xml` directly in this engagement (the fetch tool restricts to URLs surfaced during crawling). Confirm in GSC that the sitemap lists all current pages including the new builds as they ship, and that IndexNow is actually firing on publish (it is configured; verify it is succeeding in logs).

| NeuroNation | Clinical-leaning authority, EU presence | Free, lightweight, English-market casual users | Clinical/medical-partnership queries |
| Impulse | App Store volume, broad mini-game catalogue | Depth-over-breadth visual memory, honesty | Broad "brain games" catalogue terms |
| CogniFit | Assessment/clinical authority, "memory test" terms | "I just want to train, free" intent; honest non-clinical framing | Clinical assessment, condition-specific medical terms |

**Where Blanked wins on SERPs:** any query combining *free / no-subscription / honest / visual-memory-specific*. The incumbents are all subscription businesses, so they structurally cannot rank well for "free alternative" intent without undercutting their own model; and they cannot match the honesty angle without inviting the efficacy-claim scrutiny they have spent years avoiding. That is a durable moat, not a temporary gap.

**Where Blanked always loses:** brand-volume head terms, broad-cognition category terms, and clinical/efficacy terms. Do not fight these; they are won with money and decades, and on efficacy terms Blanked's own honesty correctly prevents it from competing.

**The niche to own:** "the honest, genuinely-free visual-memory trainer." Concretely, that means owning (a) the free-alternative cluster, (b) the visual-memory test/tool cluster, and (c) the "does this actually work" skeptic cluster. Win those three and Blanked has a defensible organic position that no subscription competitor can take back.
